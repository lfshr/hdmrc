About us

Our history

From an advert in the Helensburgh Advertiser in 1989 to award-winning layouts at exhibitions across Scotland and beyond: the history of the club, including its most significant events.

How it all began

In the autumn of 1989, two elderly members of the ‘Helensburgh Model Boat Builders Club’ put an advert in the ‘Helensburgh Advertiser’, looking to start a proposed model railway club in the town. These gents were war heroes Gilbert Hutchison and Archie Allan, retired Merchant Seamen who had served on the Arctic convoys of WW2, taking US and UK munitions and materiel to the Soviet Union from Faslane, known as British Military Port No 1 during the war.

In response to the advert, a meeting was held at the ‘Clyde Community Centre’ in Clyde Street, once a school and now Argyll and Bute Council area HQ. There was sufficient interest from locals as well as several Royal Navy and MOD employees based at Faslane, to start a model railway club and H&DMRC was born. The formalities were completed, a constitution agreed, the rental of a regular club room in the Clyde Centre was acquired and Geoff Dalton was elected Chairman with Mike Lovell as secretary. A club logo, based on that of the North British Locomotive Company was also created. Gilbert and Archie, along with Dave Forrest, a local retired electrical engineer, became the backbone of the club for the next decade.

Trierro, Seaforth and Craigendoran Pier

The club joined the AMRSS and its first club layout, which made its debut at Model Rail 1991, was called “Trierro” (trial and error). It was described in the Modelrail ’91 programme as ‘a continuous (oo) layout with a branch line running from station with goods avoiding line and sidings...’ Trierro was a successful first effort, and was followed by ‘Seaforth’, a larger 2 level ‘oo’ gauge looped layout.

The club was going from strength to strength, so much so that with the 100th anniversary of the West Highland main line (Glasgow to Fort William), due in 1994, the club decided, very ambitiously, to model Craigendoran Pier, as it was in the 1950s in ‘oo’ gauge. This massive undertaking took a year to complete, with Gilbert and Archie using their boatbuilding skills and Dave, his skills as an electrician, to great effect. A 4mm model of a Clyde Paddle steamer ‘Jeanie Deans’ sat at the pier and local artist, the late Neil MacLeod painted beautiful end panels on the layout that measured 30ft by 15ft.

The layout’s public debut was at the Victoria Halls ‘100 years of the WHL’ event, sponsored by Scotrail, in August 1994, as a static model, before being electrified for its operational debut at Modelrail 1995. A souvenir ticket from the show can be seen below. There, it received many plaudits, winning 2 cups, including one for scenery albeit it was not voted ‘best in show’. Craigendoran went on to appear again at Modelrail on three more occasions, the last appearance coming in 2000.

Pictures are shown below along with an original track plan. Sadly the layout had deteriorated so much that it had to be confined to the skip in 2026. The ‘Jeanie Deans’ and several of the buildings have been stored successfully.

The 2000s

After Craigendoran, club members carried out stewarding and other duties at Modelrail Scotland, which had moved to the SECC by this time, while Trierro and Seaforth layouts appeared at exhibitions in Ayr, Dumfries and elsewhere.

The club continued to support other club exhibitions and the annual AMRSS show over the years, as it still does to this day.

Craigendoran was followed by other layouts in the 2000s, namely King Street Gardens in ‘oo’ gauge, Shieldinch in ‘N’ and Levenford, an ‘N’ gauge layout running American trains, which featured at Modelrail 2014. Clyde Street, a 1950s North Clyde suburban ‘oo’ layout, based on the now closed Caledonian Railway Clyde Riverside line, featured at Modelrail 2016.

Trenches

For Modelrail 2018, the club built ‘Trenches’, a narrow gauge 4mm layout based on the supply lines of WW1. This successful layout won the Tolworth Trainshow Cup, at an exhibition held in Surrey on Armistice Day 2018, exactly 100 years after the end of WW1. The layout also featured in ‘Continental Modeller’ magazine and the ‘Helensburgh Advertiser’.

A selection of Trenches pictures are shown below. A collection for the Erskine Veterans Charity raised a significant amount at the show in Tolworth. At Model Rail Scotland 2018, WW1 Re-enactment Volunteers flanked a club member at the show, raising the profile of the layout.

Recent years

More recently, several of the club’s layouts that appear in the Our layouts section of this website, have made regular appearances at exhibitions at Paisley/Braehead, Summerlee Museum of Industrial Life, Cathcart and locations in North Ayrshire.

The club’s most recent layout, Clydemuir, a North Clyde electric lines ‘oo’ gauge layout, based on Dalmuir, made its debut at Modelrail Scotland 2026 and an article on it will be published in the March 2027 edition of ‘Railway Modeller’.

The club has always had a relaxed friendly atmosphere, where the members’ different talents, skills and interests are pulled together to ensure mutual enthusiasm and dedication to all the layouts the club has ever created. That is perhaps, one of the reasons the club is still going strong after nearly 40 years in existence.
